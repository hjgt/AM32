# S4M-LITE: isolate startup and closed-loop stop causes

User reports approximately 80% startup success with the large recorder disabled,
three audible movements on failed starts, and excessive current at 5000 RPM.
This does not establish three firmware retries or prove a specific timing error.

## Implemented

Native-early builds now retain 32 result records in `fd_light_log` (1600 bytes).
Compile-time switch `FD6288_LIGHT_LOG=0` in `Inc/fd6288_light_log.h` removes it.
No ADC waveform, extra ADC channel, per-sample logging, or closed-loop per-edge
logging is introduced. Event writes occur at start, acquisition edges/retries,
handoff, and completion. Each completion copies 48 bytes under the existing
critical section. Small overhead is not zero overhead.

Each record includes trial ID, first qualified acquisition edge, handoff time,
qualified acquisition-edge count, acquisition retry count, maximum streak,
completion reason/stage, decoded command, interval timer and filtered current
at completion. Current at completion is NOT peak current. Six-edge handoff is
software acceptance, not proof of stable mechanical rotation. Timing differences
are bounded acquisition-time DWT differences, not total long-run durations.
Reasons 10 and 11 distinguish late and invalid asynchronous commutations from
reason 9 (closed-loop missing feedback); previous codes retain their meaning.
`tools/scripts/fd6288_light_decode.cjs` decodes a stopped 1600-byte SRAM dump
located with the matching ELF's `fd_light_log` symbol. No hardware accessed yet.

## Protection review / not yet implemented

Current closed-loop stop is after >45000 interval ticks (22.5 ms) without a
qualified edge, not after one missing ADC sample. Independently, an asynchronous
commutation >50 us late stops output. Neither is a power-supply shutdown.
Existing coast, native stuck-rotor latch, safety cap and zero-throttle handling
remain unchanged. Startup timing, duty, 360 ms acquisition limit, and both
closed-loop stop conditions remain unchanged in this observation build.

Simply extending 22.5 ms would prolong energizing an unsynchronized sector.
A flight-oriented replacement needs a bounded re-acquisition state, duty cap,
real-edge requalification, finite retry/time budget, and zero-throttle/fault
preemption. It must NOT reapply stationary double alignment to a spinning rotor,
count predicted commutations as measured edges, or perpetually replenish retries.
No claim of flight readiness; current one-shot test latch is not the final policy.

Next bench comparison: same starting throttle, note failed trial number and
return to zero before reading; preserve power. Determine zero-edge failure vs
broken edge streak vs handoff-then-timeout before changing startup cadence.
Do not deliberately reproduce a high-load airborne power loss.
